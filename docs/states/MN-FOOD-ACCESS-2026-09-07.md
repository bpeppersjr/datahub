# Minnesota food licensing: location-contract assessment

Observed September 7, 2026. Decision: investigate the public food-license source before implementing acquisition. This is a separate industry candidate from Minnesota SOS Active Business Data; the historical Queue 8 SOS hold and its policy decision are unchanged. No business rows, text export, customer account, contact submission, schedule, production pointer or coverage count was created by this assessment.

## Evidence

- [MDA food-license guidance](https://www.mda.state.mn.us/food-feed/food-licenses) covers grocery/convenience stores and other food activities, while distinguishing health-department jurisdiction. It describes consolidation into Food Handler licensing from August 1, 2025. This source alone is not a statewide inventory of every food business.
- [MDA licensing liaison guidance](https://www.mda.state.mn.us/food-liaison-request) says the changes apply to existing businesses on renewal and permits businesses below $50,000 annual sales to have up to three locations on one license. **Design implication:** a license number cannot be assumed to identify exactly one physical site. This is a schema requirement, not evidence that any inspected record actually has three sites.
- The [public legacy search form](https://www2.mda.state.mn.us/webapp/lis/default.jsp?food=y) returned HTTP 200 during a direct GET. Its POST action is `LisResults.jsp`; no POST was sent. The observed `lictype` options are 57 Food Broker (selected by default), 77 Retail Food Handler, 89 Retail Mobile Food Handler, 76 Wholesale Food Handler, 62 Wholesale Food Proc/Manu and 61 Wholesale Food Proc/Manu - USDA. `countycode` is multi-select. Checked `unexpiredonly` and `holderonly` controls and a text-download button exist. The form also accepts a person's surname. These observations do not establish the exported schema, address role, completeness, allowed automated use or privacy safety.
- The [MDA license locator](https://www.mda.state.mn.us/licenses?tid=116) has a July 29, 2026 system-transition notice. The [transition page](https://www.mda.state.mn.us/licenses-permits-certificates-mda) describes customer account/application functions; it does not establish a public bulk-data replacement. Do not create an applicant account to obtain business inventory.

The legacy choices and current classification guidance are not equivalent. It remains unverified whether old selections include renewed Food Handler licenses, omit them, or map internally to new classes. A successful HTTP response would not resolve this coverage question.

A parallel source reviewer identified useful counterevidence, independently checked by the integrator: the current [official lookup landing page](https://www.mda.state.mn.us/licensing/license-lookup) still directs food searches to the legacy host. The transition notice does **not** prove that food search is retired. The [modernization timeline](https://www.mda.state.mn.us/food-licensing-modernization) phases existing manufacturer/processor renewals from January 1, 2026, mobile renewals from April 1 and retail/wholesale renewals from July 1; converted retail/wholesale licenses move to full-calendar renewal January 1, 2027. A type or renewal-period change alone is not evidence of business closure or opening.

## Required connector contract and next action

1. Locate the publisher's current public lookup/export documentation and automation/retention/redistribution terms. Establish whether the legacy endpoint remains the authoritative selected view after the transition. Prefer documented public delivery over UI automation. If public documentation cannot resolve it, prepare a publisher inquiry; do not send it without authority.
2. Obtain a bounded, privacy-selected schema observation only after its access basis is established. Resolve license, holder, establishment and location keys; their lifecycle; premises versus mailing addresses; status/date meanings; and legacy-to-current type mapping. Neither `holderonly` nor `unexpiredonly` proves a physical operating business.
3. Preserve license-to-location relationships. Keep fixed, mobile, broker, warehouse and manufacturing activities distinct; do not map every Food Handler to grocery retail. Unknown subtype stays unknown. Keep source status separate from inferred operational status and observation time separate from publisher dates.
4. Require selected-view count/size controls, stable pagination or an atomic export, change/deletion semantics and drift checks. Never use the default broker selection as a complete food inventory. Reconcile jurisdiction coverage separately from MDH/local records and existing national-source evidence.
5. Before enrollment, implement offline tests for one license/multiple sites, multiple licenses/one site, classification transitions, mobile/non-premises records, missing identifiers, status ambiguity, truncation, retry/deadline/cancellation and atomic publication. Store only address lat/lon on business sites; ZIP5 and ZIP4 remain separate. Do not derive site coordinates from county centroids or inspector territories.
6. After validation and implementation, Co*Tive workers own acquisition, receipts, retries and scheduling. Agents do not remain attached to downloads. No Minnesota food connector or recurring job is ready from this form inspection alone.
